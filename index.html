How your commission works

A quick guide — no finance background needed.

The big idea

You earn a commission on every recurring-revenue (RMR) agreement you sell — and again when it renews. Your commission is built from three things: how big the deal is, a percentage set by the contract length, and a multiplier based on how profitable the deal is.

Commission = Deal value × Term % × Margin multiplier. Every rate below is set by your administrator, so the numbers always reflect the plan assigned to you.

Auto-renewals pay a lower rate because they take less sales effort, and only 12 and 24-month agreements qualify. 36/48/60-month deals aren't auto-renewal eligible — they're expected to be renegotiated.

Put it together — an example

Renewals — how each agreement type behaves

Every agreement is one of four types, set when it's created, which decides its renewal commission:

TypeRenewal commission
Manual RenewalHigher manual rate, every cycle with no limit — a 12-month deal renewed yearly pays every year. Each cycle is its own line with its own bonus quarter and full history, and it re-surfaces in Pending manual renewals each time it's due.
Auto-RenewalLower auto rate, one time only (12 / 24-month deals only). Never appears in Pending Renewals.
One-Time / Month-to-MonthNo renewal commission.

Renegotiated an auto-renewal (new pricing, term, or a fresh signed agreement)? Use Convert to manual on the auto-renewals table — it switches that renewal to the higher manual rate, since you earned it. A deal under 45% margin pays $0 and is flagged Management Approval Required.

When you get paid — your commission over time

On a new agreement, your commission doesn't all land at once. It's split so your pay follows the deal actually performing. The total is exactly the same — it's just spread out:

PieceHow muchWhen it lands
Initial — paid nowThe plan's initial share of the commission (workbook plans: 50%)The quarter you sell it
DeferredThe remaining share (workbook plans: 50%)Workbook plans: about 3 months after first billing (installed and invoiced). Older plans: once the customer has paid 25% of the first year
Accelerator
the high-margin bonus (the ×-above-1.0 part)
the margin bumpIn 6-month pieces, each released as that window's revenue is collected

The holds protect against early cancellation and confirm profitability — if a payment is missed, that piece waits until enough is collected. Renewals aren't held — they pay in full when renewed. Deals sold before 2026, and anything already paid, pay 100% up front unchanged.

Renewal Forecast & Pending Renewals

The Renewal Forecast tab shows what each upcoming renewal is worth — the estimated bonus, the math behind it, and reminder dates. It's informational only and never creates a payout. Pending Renewals is where you actually renew: it holds the two action tables (manual and auto) with the Renew button.
